The Project

Digital Lucretius is a digital scholarly edition prototype of Lucretius’ De Rerum Natura focusing on the proem to Book III. Based on the critical edition established by Marcus Deufert (2019), it enables users to explore and compare the different manuscript witnesses alongside the editorial choices of major classical scholars.

This project was developed by Silvia Antonella Pellicano—who was responsible for the XML/TEI encoding and philological annotations—for the course Digital Scholarly Editing: Theory, Methods and Practice at the Alma Mater Studiorum – Università di Bologna.

Introduction

The Work

De Rerum Natura ("On the Nature of Things") is a didactic poem in six books, composed by Titus Lucretius Carus in the first century BCE to expound the physics and ethics of Epicurean philosophy. The proem to Book III turns from the atomic foundations of the universe to the fear of death, and to the figure of Epicurus who first showed a way beyond it.

Author

Titus Lucretius Carus (c. 99–55 BCE) remains one of the most elusive poets of the late Roman Republic, known almost entirely through the single poem he left behind.

Historical Context

The poem was composed during the final, turbulent decades of the Republic, a period of civil war whose anxieties shape Lucretius' Epicurean call to tranquillity.

Manuscript Witnesses

Digital Edition

Open Edition

The edition presents Deufert’s critical text of the proem to Book III (vv. 1–93) with its three apparatuses (apparatus criticus, testimonia and loci paralleli) always visible below the text. Readers can switch on further layers: the lemmatization of every word, the philosophical keywords and Slavitt’s English translation.

Encoding

The text is encoded in XML according to the TEI P5 Guidelines, with the critical apparatus in parallel segmentation, every word lemmatized and every siglum and name linked to its witness or scholar. The file is validated against the TEI schema.

Read more

The Website

The edition page is generated from the TEI file with an XSLT 3.0 stylesheet run with Saxon and published as static HTML, together with companion pages for the keywords, the scholars and sources, and an interactive stemma codicum.

Read more

Features & Limitations

The edition colour-codes the evidence and links the text to its sources. As a prototype, it covers only 93 lines, depends on Deufert’s apparatus without an independent collation, and does not yet offer full-text search or a synchronised translation.
